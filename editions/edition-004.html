Patch now: the top 3

High

1. October Patch Tuesday lands today, October 13

The October Patch Tuesday updates are out today. Every month the same advice applies: the bad guys reverse-engineer these patches within days, so the clock starts now. Prioritize internet-facing and authenticated-remote-access systems first, then workstations, then everything else. If you still have items lingering from last month's cycle, this is the week they become the real risk, not the new patches.

Who is affected: every shop running Windows, plus the usual accompanying updates from Adobe and the browser vendors. If your fleet auto-patches, your job is verification, not the install itself.

Action: Start patching today. Clear last month's leftovers first, confirm your fleet's update reporting is actually reporting, and schedule the server maintenance window before the end of the week.
High

2. Cisco Secure Client VPN browser-based auth goes live today

This is the one flagged in last week's edition: Cisco Secure Client VPN sign-in shifts to a browser window or tab using SSO and Microsoft MFA starting today. The key nuance stands: the My Sign-Ins default method no longer governs VPN authentication; Microsoft Entra ID picks the method from whatever each user has registered. Users with stale or missing MFA methods will hit a wall at the new flow with no obvious fix, and the browser redirect will look like a phishing attempt to anyone who was not told about it.

Who is affected: shops running Cisco Secure Client with Entra SSO and Microsoft MFA. Everyone else gets a free lesson in how SSO vendors keep moving auth into the browser.

Action: If you did not audit registered MFA methods last week, do it today. Send the heads-up describing the legitimate browser flow, and staff the helpdesk for the ticket bump that lands with it.
Plan

3. Mid-October phishing check-in: awareness month is half over

Cybersecurity Awareness Month is halfway through, and the AI-phishing wave from last week's edition has not slowed. The lures now look personal and grammatical, and deepfake voice and video are turning up in wire-transfer and gift-card scams. If you sent one awareness note at the start of the month, the midpoint is the right time for the follow-up: no money moves and no credential sharing on a single channel, ever, and urgent requests get verified through a second channel.

Who is affected: every organization with email and a finance department, which is all of them.

Action: Send the mid-month reminder this week. While you are at it, review your MFA registrations: stolen sessions are how these lures get cashed out.
