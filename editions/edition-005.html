Patch now: the top 3

High

1. Cisco Secure Client browser-auth: one week in

Cisco shifted VPN sign-in to a browser window or tab using SSO and Microsoft MFA starting October 13. This was a prep item two editions ago and a launch-day item last edition; now it is a week-in-review item. The key nuance still stands: Entra picks the authentication method from whatever each user has registered, not from the My Sign-Ins default. Users with stale or missing MFA methods hit a wall at the new flow with no obvious fix, and the browser redirect still looks like a phishing attempt to anyone who was not told about it.

Who is affected: shops running Cisco Secure Client with Entra SSO and Microsoft MFA. Everyone else gets another free lesson in how SSO vendors keep moving auth into the browser.

Action: Check your helpdesk ticket trend for this week. A small bump means your user comms worked; a big one means you need a second round showing what the legitimate browser flow looks like. If you never audited registered MFA methods, do it now, because the users who hit walls last week are still hitting them.
High

2. October Patch Tuesday stragglers, one week in

The October updates landed October 13. One week later, the exposure math has changed: the patches are public, which means attackers have had seven days to reverse-engineer them and build working exploits. Any system still unpatched from the October 13 cycle is now the soft target. One week of delay on a known patch is when the risk curve gets steep, and stragglers from this cycle become the targets of next month's scanning campaigns.

Who is affected: every shop running Windows, plus the accompanying updates from Adobe and the browser vendors. If your fleet auto-patches, your job is verification, not the install itself.

Action: Verify that your patch reporting is actually truthful, because tools that claim everything is current are not always telling you the truth. Clear last week's leftovers first, then confirm your server maintenance windows actually happened and covered every system they were supposed to.
Plan

3. IT/OT convergence takes the awareness-month spotlight

Purdue's IT newsroom published "Protect critical systems and your data" on October 14 as its Cybersecurity Awareness Month piece on IT/OT convergence. The core message: IT and operational technology are converging, and the same hygiene rules apply on both sides. Patch your devices. Keep personal devices off research networks. Report odd behavior to your security team. Back up before ransomware, not after.

Who is affected: any organization with research networks, operational technology, or users who mix personal devices with work systems. That is a bigger share of shops than most admins want to admit, because lab equipment, industrial controls, and building systems tend to live in a blind spot the regular patch cycle never touches.

Action: If you run anything that looks like operational technology, make sure it is on somebody's patch list and not sitting in a forgotten subnet. The article also links a BTAA session on the state of critical infrastructure, worth a skim for the sector context.

The month ends October 31, and the AI-phishing wave flagged two editions ago has not slowed: the lures look personal and grammatical, and deepfake voice and video are turning up in wire-transfer and gift-card scams. If you have only sent one or two awareness notes this month, the final week is your last clean shot at a third.

Action: Same message as before: no money moves and no credential sharing on a single channel, ever, and urgent requests get verified through a second channel.
